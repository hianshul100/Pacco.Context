# Validated Intent

We want to provide a **Pacco Delivery Rescheduling and Delivery Preferences** capability that lets an authenticated customer view an existing eligible delivery and its current schedule, retrieve alternative delivery days available on the delivery's **already-assigned resource**, select and confirm a new calendar day, and add or update delivery instructions — releasing the previously held slot only once the new slot is reserved,
to achieve a simple self-service delivery-management experience that removes manual support intervention from routine delivery-schedule changes while keeping delivery availability accurate,
measured by:
- An authenticated customer can open an eligible delivery and see its current schedule and status correctly.
- Alternative delivery days on the delivery's currently assigned resource can be retrieved, and an alternative day can be selected and confirmed.
- A successful reschedule updates the order's delivery date to the newly selected calendar day and the delivery reflects it; the newly reserved slot stops being available to conflicting bookings at the same or lower reservation priority; and the previous slot becomes available again where applicable.
- A reschedule whose target day is already held at equal or higher priority is rejected (`cannot_expropriate_reservation`) with a customer-readable "day no longer available" message, and the original schedule and reservation are left intact.
- A delivery whose newly held slot is later expropriated by a strictly higher-priority reservation is surfaced to the customer as "schedule lost — needs rescheduling" on their next read of that delivery, rather than displaying a schedule it no longer holds.
- Delivery instructions can be saved and displayed correctly.
- Rescheduling history records the previous and new schedules.
- A customer cannot reschedule a delivery they do not own, and a delivery is rejected for rescheduling whenever its order is Completed or Canceled, its delivery is Completed or Failed, or its currently scheduled delivery date is not in the future.
- A slot that becomes unavailable before confirmation produces a clear, customer-readable failure message rather than a system error.
- Concurrent reschedule requests do not create duplicate or conflicting reservations, and service failures leave the delivery in a consistent state.
- No cancellation, payment, notification, vehicle reassignment, or unrelated delivery functionality is introduced.

within the existing Pacco delivery and availability domain — anchored on **CAP-09 Delivery Execution & Tracking** (Pacco.Services.Deliveries) and **CAP-04 Resource Availability & Reservation** (Pacco.Services.Availability), with **CAP-07 Order Lifecycle Management** (Pacco.Services.Orders) as the authoritative holder of the order's delivery date — reached through the existing Pacco API Gateway and service boundaries, with a bookable slot defined as a **calendar day on the delivery's currently assigned resource** for the first release, and explicitly excluding intra-day delivery time windows (deferred to a later increment), resource/vehicle reassignment and cross-resource slot search, delivery cancellation, address change, order or delivery creation, payments and rescheduling fees, driver reassignment, administrator and automatic rescheduling, AI-based slot recommendation, SMS/email/push notification, recurring scheduling, and any change to the existing authentication or authorization model,
under constraints of: only authenticated customers may reschedule, and no customer may reschedule another customer's delivery; delivery ownership and eligibility are validated by backend services and not only by the UI, and the ownership guard must deny an empty or unauthenticated caller context rather than pass it through, because ADR-006 places the authentication boundary at the edge and leaves services' own handlers open — a bypass that is already a live defect across four handlers in two repositories; a delivery is eligible only when its order is not Completed or Canceled, its delivery is not Completed or Failed, and its currently scheduled delivery date is still in the future, with no additional lead-time cutoff in the first release, and invalid or unsupported delivery states must not silently proceed; the order's delivery date remains the authoritative schedule and the delivery record reflects it rather than holding a competing schedule of record; a slot shown as available is revalidated at confirmation and the UI must not assume availability from previously loaded data; a slot must not be double-booked by concurrent requests; the existing slot is released only as part of a consistent rescheduling operation in which the new slot is successfully reserved; **the bookable set is the delivery's currently assigned resource only — only the calendar day changes and the assigned vehicle never changes**, so no slot-eligibility scheme may introduce resource reassignment or cross-resource search; **a customer-initiated reschedule reserves at one fixed standard customer-reschedule priority, identical for every customer**, so it never expropriates an incumbent and is rejected on collision with first-come-wins semantics; CAP-04 reserves a resource against a calendar date with an integer priority and resolves collisions by **priority expropriation** — a strictly higher priority evicts the incumbent, equal priority does not expropriate and first-come wins, raising `cannot_expropriate_reservation` — so reschedule behaviour and its acceptance criteria are defined against that rule rather than assuming an exclusive hold; **a confirmed reschedule may still be expropriated later by a strictly higher-priority reservation, and that eviction is accepted rather than prevented**, with the delivery surfacing as "schedule lost — needs rescheduling" on the customer's next read; resource tags are immutable for a resource's life (changing them requires deleting and recreating the resource, which changes its id and cancels its reservations), so no slot-eligibility scheme may depend on re-tagging existing resources; persistence changes are additive and backward-compatible — fields added, never renamed or removed, enum ordinals append-only — because the platform has no migration tooling (ADR-008); collaboration stays choreographed by default, with order creation remaining the single recorded orchestrated process (ADR-011), and synchronous service-to-service calls remain single-entity reads that gate a write, never chained and never returning an unbounded collection (ADR-010); raw exceptions, internal service details and stack traces are never shown to customers; delivery instructions cannot be used to bypass delivery validation or scheduling rules; and the feature uses the existing Pacco API Gateway and service boundaries, the existing edge identity binding (`customerId` bound from `@user_id`), the per-service RabbitMQ exchange convention recorded in ADR-001, and the platform runtime baseline pinned by ADR-020.

## Assumptions

- **ASM-1 (Scope, inferred):** The capability anchors to CAP-09 Delivery Execution & Tracking and CAP-04 Resource Availability & Reservation, with CAP-07 Order Lifecycle Management as the authoritative schedule holder. No new bounded context or deployable is introduced; the catalog surfaces no separate "delivery scheduling" capability — the nearest named capabilities are CAP-04's *Reserve a resource for a date* and *Release a reservation*.
- **ASM-2 (Constraints, inferred):** Authentication and authorization are additive to the existing edge model — the gateway's `customerId`/`@user_id` binding remains the identity source and no new auth scheme is introduced, consistent with the intent's explicit exclusion of auth-model changes.
- **ASM-3 (Signals, inferred):** The stated success signals are read as acceptance-level behavioural checks, not instrumented KPIs. No adoption, deflection, or support-ticket-reduction targets were stated and none are invented here.
- **ASM-4 (Scope, inferred):** "The previous slot becomes available again where applicable" is read as: release the prior reservation only when the reschedule succeeds **and** the prior slot was actually held on this delivery's behalf — never as an unconditional release.
- **ASM-5 (NFR assumed — NFR-11):** Customer-entered delivery instructions are length-bounded and excluded from verbatim log sinks. The deliveries-service already carries an unbounded caller-supplied text field written verbatim to console, file and Seq; repeating that shape for a free-text customer field would be a privacy regression.
- **ASM-6 (NFR assumed — NFR-12):** New customer-facing reschedule screens meet WCAG 2.1 AA. No accessibility standard is recorded anywhere in the Pacco catalog and the platform has no first-party frontend beyond the `Pacco.Web` placeholder recorded by ADR-021, so an industry-default bar is assumed rather than discovered.
- **ASM-7 (NFR assumed — NFR-13):** Every reschedule attempt and outcome is traceable end to end by the existing correlation / operation identifier already used across the gateway, availability-service and operations-service.
- **ASM-8 (Constraints, inferred):** The feature ships on the currently pinned runtime and toolkit baseline (.NET Core 3.1 / Convey 0.4 line per ADR-020 and ADR-002, with every pipeline pinning the same SDK patch version); runtime modernization is out of scope for this intent even though the baseline is past end of support.
- **ASM-9 (Scope — decision, resolved with Product):** A bookable "delivery slot" is a **calendar day** for the first release. Intra-day time windows are explicitly deferred to a later increment and are out of scope here. This aligns with CAP-04, whose Reservation is keyed on calendar day.
- **ASM-10 (Scope — decision, resolved with Product):** **Orders** holds the authoritative delivery schedule: the order's delivery date stays authoritative and the delivery record reflects it. A successful reschedule therefore changes the order's delivery date, and the delivery must not become a second, competing system of record for the schedule.
- **ASM-11 (Constraints — decision, resolved with Product):** Eligibility for rescheduling is: the associated order is not Completed or Canceled, **and** the delivery is not Completed or Failed, **and** the currently scheduled delivery date is still in the future. No additional lead-time cutoff applies in the first release.
- **ASM-12 (Scope, inferred):** Adding or updating delivery instructions is an independent customer action available while the delivery is still eligible — it is not gated behind a reschedule. The source lists instruction update as its own in-scope outcome with its own acceptance signal, separate from the slot-change flow.
- **ASM-13 (Scope — decision, resolved with Product):** Rescheduling history is an append-only record on the delivery; no history edit, delete, or purge behaviour is in scope. Its visibility and retention are decided (OQ-1): the history is **audit-only on the delivery record with full retention** — kept for audit and support, with no separate customer-facing rescheduling-history view in the first release.
- **ASM-14 (Constraints — decision, resolved with Product):** A customer-initiated reschedule reserves at **one fixed standard customer-reschedule priority, equal for every customer**; on collision it is rejected and first-come wins. Because CAP-04's equal-priority rule only yields first-come-wins against reservations held at the *same* integer, this priority is taken to be the same standard customer reservation priority already used by the existing booking path, so a reschedule can neither expropriate nor be expropriated by an ordinary customer booking.
- **ASM-15 (Scope — decision, resolved with Product):** The bookable set offered as "alternative delivery slots" is the **delivery's currently assigned resource only**. Only the calendar day changes; the assigned vehicle never changes. Cross-resource search, resource reassignment, and any re-tagging scheme are out of scope — consistent with CAP-04 resource tags being immutable for a resource's life.
- **ASM-16 (Scope — decision, resolved with Product):** A later strictly-higher-priority reservation **may** expropriate a customer's newly held slot. That eviction is accepted rather than prevented; the delivery is surfaced as **"schedule lost — needs rescheduling"** on the customer's next read of that delivery. This introduces a new delivery-side state indicator, which must be added additively per NFR-17.
- **ASM-17 (Constraints — decision, resolved with Product/Ops):** There is **no cap on how many times a delivery may be rescheduled and no cool-off between attempts** in the first release (OQ-2). The eligibility rule in ASM-11 is the only limit; a cap can be introduced later if abuse or operational concerns emerge, without changing the workflow shape.
- **ASM-18 (Constraints — decision, resolved with Architecture):** Reschedule confirmation **inherits the edge write mode already configured for the target environment's gateway** and introduces no feature-specific mode (OQ-3). Where the gateway is synchronous the result returns synchronously; where it is asynchronous the existing `202 Accepted` plus operation-status pattern applies, and the outcome must be observable within the operation-status sliding expiry recorded by NFR-9.
- **ASM-19 (Scope — decision, resolved with Product):** The alternative-day list returns **the next 14 eligible calendar days, ordered ascending by date** (OQ-4), restricted to days actually available on the delivery's currently assigned resource. This satisfies ADR-010's Constraint C10 prohibition on a synchronous read returning an unbounded collection.
- **ASM-20 (Constraints — decision, resolved with Architecture):** A later expropriation of the customer's slot (ASM-16) is detected by **revalidating the delivery's current reservation against CAP-04 at delivery read time** (OQ-5). When no reservation remains for the expected resource and delivery date, the delivery reports "schedule lost — needs rescheduling". The reservation event contract is **not** changed in the first release, so `resource_reservation_canceled` is not enriched and no cross-service contract change governed by ADR-003 is introduced.

## Open Questions

All open questions below were answered by the reviewer and are resolved; no question remains outstanding.

- **OQ-1 [Product] — Resolved:** Is rescheduling history customer-visible or audit-only, and how many prior schedule changes are retained? **Decision: (a) audit-only on the delivery record, with full retention.** Customers do not need a separate rescheduling-history view in the first release; the history is kept for audit and support purposes. Carried into ASM-13, DO1 and DO2.
- **OQ-2 [Product/Ops] — Resolved:** How many times may a single delivery be rescheduled, and is there a cool-off between attempts? **Decision: no hard cap and no cool-off period for the first release.** The existing eligibility rules (ASM-11) are enough; a limit can be introduced later if the business identifies abuse or operational concerns. Carried into ASM-17 and DO2.
- **OQ-3 [Architecture] — Resolved:** Does reschedule confirmation return synchronously, or over the asynchronous edge as `202 Accepted` plus an operation id the client polls? **Decision: use the existing gateway write mode configured for the target environment; do not introduce a feature-specific mode.** If the gateway is synchronous, return the result synchronously; if it is asynchronous, use the existing `202 Accepted` plus operation-status pattern — consistent with ADR-014 constraining the edge write mode to a whole-gateway configuration selection rather than a per-route one. Carried into ASM-18 and DO2.
- **OQ-4 [Product] — Resolved:** What forward horizon and ordering apply to the alternative-day list shown for the delivery's assigned resource? **Decision: return the next 14 eligible calendar days, ordered ascending by date.** The result is bounded and only days actually available on the currently assigned resource are returned, satisfying ADR-010's Constraint C10 prohibition on an unbounded synchronous read. Carried into ASM-19 and DO1.
- **OQ-5 [Architecture] — Resolved:** How is a later expropriation of the customer's slot (ASM-16) detected so the delivery can report "schedule lost"? **Decision: (a) revalidate the current reservation against CAP-04 when the delivery is read.** If the reservation no longer exists for the expected resource and delivery date, surface "schedule lost — needs rescheduling". The reservation event contract is not changed in the first release, so `resource_reservation_canceled` is not enriched and no ADR-003-governed cross-service contract change is introduced. Carried into ASM-20 and DO1.

## Non-Functional Requirements

- NFR-1 [security]: Only authenticated customers can reschedule, and delivery ownership plus eligibility are enforced server-side rather than by the UI — target: 100% of reschedule and instruction-update operations carry server-side ownership and eligibility checks (source: requirements_doc, confidence: high)
- NFR-2 [security]: The reschedule ownership guard denies an unauthenticated or empty caller context instead of passing it through — target: fail closed on empty caller context (source: cake:security, confidence: high)
- NFR-3 [concurrency]: A delivery slot is never double-booked by concurrent confirmations, and availability is revalidated at confirmation time — target: zero double-booked slots under concurrent confirm requests (source: requirements_doc, confidence: high)
- NFR-4 [concurrency]: The reschedule write path must not depend on the platform's existing optimistic-concurrency behaviour and must not lose a competing update silently — target: no silent lost update on concurrent reschedule of the same delivery (source: cake:concurrency, confidence: high)
- NFR-5 [data_integrity]: The previous slot is released only once the new slot is reserved; a failed reschedule leaves the delivery, the order's delivery date, and both reservations in their pre-request state — target: zero orphaned or double-held reservations after a failed reschedule (source: requirements_doc, confidence: high)
- NFR-6 [data_integrity]: A submitted slot value is never silently truncated or reinterpreted; intra-day precision the reservation store cannot hold is rejected rather than discarded — target: zero silently discarded time components (source: cake:data_integrity, confidence: high)
- NFR-7 [reliability_resilience]: Reschedule command handlers rely on the platform's transactional inbox/outbox decorator for de-duplication rather than hand-rolled retry logic, so a redelivered command does not produce a second reservation — target: zero duplicate reservations from redelivery within the de-duplication window (source: cake:reliability_resilience, confidence: high)
- NFR-8 [performance]: Schedule-change events reach the owning service's exchange within the recorded outbox dispatch interval — target: ~2 s (source: cake:performance, confidence: medium)
- NFR-9 [performance]: When confirmation runs over the asynchronous edge, the reschedule outcome is observable before the operation-status record expires — target: outcome available within the 300-second operation-status sliding expiry (source: cake:performance, confidence: high)
- NFR-10 [security]: No raw exception text, internal service detail, or stack trace appears in any customer-facing response — target: zero internal detail in customer-facing error payloads (source: requirements_doc, confidence: high)
- NFR-11 [privacy_compliance]: Customer-entered delivery instructions are length-bounded and excluded from verbatim log sinks — target: bounded length; not written verbatim to console/file/Seq (source: assumption, confidence: medium)
- NFR-12 [usability_accessibility]: New customer-facing reschedule screens meet a recognised accessibility bar — target: WCAG 2.1 AA (source: assumption, confidence: low)
- NFR-13 [observability]: Every reschedule attempt and outcome is traceable across gateway, deliveries, availability and orders by the existing correlation / operation identifier — target: 100% of reschedule attempts correlated end to end (source: assumption, confidence: medium)
- NFR-14 [usability_accessibility]: Every rejected reschedule returns a distinct, human-readable reason that separates "day no longer available", "held at an equal or higher priority", "not eligible", "not owned", and system error — target: a distinct customer-readable reason for each rejection class (source: requirements_doc, confidence: high)
- NFR-15 [maintainability]: New reschedule messages are published on the owning service's own exchange using the platform's snakeCase naming, and the routing key matches the command type name and is registered in the platform message manifest — target: 100% of new messages registered, name-matched, and published on the owning service's exchange (source: cake:maintainability, confidence: high)
- NFR-16 [portability_infra]: The feature is delivered on the currently pinned platform runtime and toolkit baseline with no runtime upgrade, and every pipeline pins the same SDK patch version — target: no deviation from the pinned .NET Core 3.1 / Convey 0.4 baseline (source: cake:portability_infra, confidence: high)
- NFR-17 [data_integrity]: Persistence changes for rescheduling history, delivery instructions, and the "schedule lost" indicator are additive and backward-compatible — fields added, never renamed or removed, enum ordinals append-only — because the platform has no migration tooling — target: zero renamed/removed persistence fields and zero renumbered enum ordinals (source: cake:data_integrity, confidence: high)
- NFR-18 [operational_readiness]: Reschedule tests must actually execute in the pipeline and block the published image on failure, not merely compile — target: reschedule test suite executed on every build, failing build on failure (source: cake:operational_readiness, confidence: medium)
- NFR-19 [scalability]: The alternative-day retrieval returns a bounded, paged collection and never an unbounded list — target: every slot-availability read returns a bounded page (source: cake:scalability, confidence: high)
- NFR-20 [availability]: Reschedule confirmation inherits the reservation path's synchronous dependency on customers-service; that dependency must degrade to a clear customer-readable failure rather than an unhandled error — target: customers-service unavailability yields a customer-readable retryable failure, never a partial reschedule (source: cake:availability, confidence: medium)
- NFR-21 [observability]: Outbox depth and oldest-message age for the new reschedule messages are observable and alertable before the expiry window, so a stuck schedule-change message is not silently discarded — target: depth and oldest-message-age signals emitted and alerted ahead of the expiry window (source: cake:observability, confidence: medium)
- NFR-22 [security]: Reschedule-driven reserve and release commands carry the owning customer's identity as bound at the edge, so the reservation path's own caller check passes for the owner and rejects everyone else — target: zero reserve/release commands issued without the gateway-bound owning `customerId` (source: cake:security, confidence: high)

# Input required recap

## Questions and answers

### 1. Reservation priority and eviction on the target day

**Question:** What reservation priority does a customer-initiated reschedule use, and may it evict an existing booking on the target day?

**Answer:** Use one fixed standard customer-reschedule priority for every customer, equal across customers — rejected on collision, first-come wins.

### 2. Bookable set for alternative delivery slots
